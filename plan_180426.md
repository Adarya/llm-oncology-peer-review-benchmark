## 1. Background & Rationale

*(Unchanged from v1.)*

Peer review in biomedical publishing faces mounting strain from rising submission volumes and reviewer fatigue. Large language models have been proposed as a scalable adjunct, but their accuracy, reliability, and biases remain incompletely characterized — especially in specialized clinical domains.

### Prior work this study builds on
- **Liang et al. 2024 (NEJM AI)** — GPT-4 on 3,096 Nature + 1,709 ICLR papers; ~30–39% overlap with human reviewers.
- **Shen et al. 2026 (JMIR AI)** — 5 open-source LLMs on 200 transplantation papers; best exact-match accuracy 0.35.
- **Joachim et al. 2025 (J Oral Maxillofac Surg)** — 4 frontier models on 22 OMFS papers; AI rejection rates 0–9% vs. 68% human (leniency).
- **Li et al. 2026 (N Biotechnol)** — GPT-5, Qwen-Plus, Gemini 2.5 Pro on 763 biotech preprints; lenient, weak at positioning critique.
- **Suleiman et al. 2024 (Comput Methods Programs Biomed)** — 78.5% of human comments had no agreement with GPT-4 in medical journal.

### Gap this study addresses
1. No oncology-specific benchmark exists.
2. No systematic test of 2026 frontier models (Claude Opus 4.7, Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5).
3. Most prior work used single-agent architectures; multi-agent decomposition untested in biomedical peer review.
4. Training-data contamination rarely controlled explicitly.
5. Oncology has well-structured reporting standards (CONSORT, REMARK, STROBE), clear LoE hierarchies, and strong journal stratification — uniquely amenable to rigorous benchmarking.

## 2. Research Questions & Hypotheses

### Primary research question
How accurately and reliably can frontier LLM agents assess the quality and publishability of oncology manuscripts when presented in fully-blinded pre-submission format, and how well does their evaluation agree with blinded expert human re-review and with the actual journal tier of publication?

### Hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Prior basis |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | LLM agents will achieve moderate agreement (weighted κ > 0.4) with blinded expert re-reviewers on overall quality. | Liang 2024 |
| H2 | LLM agents will systematically over-predict publishability tier (leniency). | Joachim 2025, Li 2026, Shen 2026 |
| H3 | Multi-agent architectures will outperform single-agent on agreement with experts. | Novel |
| H4 | Agents will perform better on methodological assessment than on novelty/impact. | Liang 2024 |
| H5 | Performance will vary by study type (best on RCTs; worst on basic/translational science). | Novel |
| H6 (exploratory) | Model performance will differ significantly between Claude Opus 4.7, Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and GPT-5. | Novel for oncology |

---

## 3. Study Design

Prospective, cross-sectional, comparative diagnostic-accuracy study. Manuscripts are processed into a uniform blinded pre-submission format and evaluated by multiple LLM multi-agent pipelines and a blinded-expert human gold-standard subset.

## 4. Corpus Construction

### 4.1 Source database
- **Primary**: PubMed Central Open Access Subset (PMC-OAS) — provides clean JATS XML enabling deterministic, reproducible preprocessing.
- **Fallback** (only if tier stratification cannot be met from PMC-OAS alone): institutional-access full-text retrieval for paywalled Tier-1/2 papers, with manual QA of the extraction.

### 4.2 Inclusion criteria
- Primary research articles in oncology (MeSH: "Neoplasms" + descendant terms, or journal-category oncology)
- Published on or after **February 1, 2026** (post-dates Claude Opus 4.7 training cutoff of Jan 2026)
- English language
- Available as JATS XML in PMC-OAS, or as full-text from publisher API with structured markup
- Contains abstract, methods, results sections

### 4.3 Exclusion criteria
- Editorials, commentaries, letters, errata, corrections
- Conference abstracts
- Case reports (separate analysis, out of primary scope)
- Review articles (different rubric applies; separate study)
- Manuscripts < 1,500 words
- Papers where preprocessing cannot guarantee full blinding (see §8)

### 4.4 Stratification targets

**Journal tier** (Clarivate JIF, Oncology category, 2025 edition):

| Tier | JIF range | Example journals | Target % |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ≥ 30 | NEJM, Lancet, Lancet Oncology, Nature Cancer, Cancer Cell, Cancer Discovery, JAMA Oncology | 15–20% |
| 2 | 10–29 | JCO, Annals of Oncology, JNCI, Clinical Cancer Research, J ImmunoTher Cancer, Nature Communications (oncology subset) | 20–25% |
| 3 | 4–9.9 | Cancer, European J Cancer, Oncologist, Int J Cancer, Cancer Research Communications, BMC Cancer | 25–30% |
| 4 | < 4 | Various mid-low-IF oncology journals | 15–20% |
| 5 (control) | Predatory / low-reputation | Beall's-list-derived | 10% |

Note: PMC-OAS availability within a 2.5-month window may under-represent Tier 1–2 (embargoes up to 12 months under NIH public-access policy). If stratification cannot be met, we will either (a) extend the time window earlier (noting Gemini 3.1 Pro contamination risk for Feb 2025–Jan 2026 papers), or (b) use institutional-access fallback for paywalled Tier-1/2 papers. Final decision at corpus-freeze.

**Cancer domain** — balanced representation:
breast · lung · GI · GU · hematologic · CNS · gynecologic · head & neck · sarcoma · melanoma/skin · pediatric · rare/NOS

**Study type** — balanced representation:
- Phase III / late-phase RCT
- Phase I–II / early-phase trial
- Observational (cohort, case-control, cross-sectional)
- Meta-analysis / systematic review
- Translational / biomarker
- Preclinical / basic science
- Real-world / registry

**Negative controls** (separate from tier stratification):
- ~30 retracted papers (via Retraction Watch, published post-Feb 2026)
- ~30 predatory-journal papers

### 4.5 Sample size
- **Minimum n = 400** (powers primary weighted κ = 0.4 vs. null 0.2 at α = 0.05, power = 0.8)
- **Target n = 600–1,000** for subgroup power (≥ 50 per tier × study-type cell)

## 6. Execution Architecture

### 6.1 Overview
Two parallel execution pipelines that both emit output conforming to a shared JSON schema (see §6.5), enabling downstream joint analysis.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Claude Code (Max plan)                                     │
│   Orchestrator agent (Opus 4.7)                            │
│     ├─ Specialist sub-agent 1: Methodological Rigor        │
│     ├─ Specialist sub-agent 2: Clinical Relevance / LoE    │
│     ├─ Specialist sub-agent 3: Reporting Quality           │
│     ├─ Specialist sub-agent 4: Novelty / Literature        │
│     ├─ Specialist sub-agent 5: Writing / Presentation      │
│     └─ Chief Reviewer synthesis                            │
│                                                            │
│   Separate run: same structure with Sonnet 4.6             │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                            ↓
              [ Shared JSON output schema ]
                            ↑
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Google Antigravity                                         │
│   Orchestrator agent (Gemini 3.1 Pro)                      │
│     └─ Same 5-specialist + chief pattern                   │
│                                                            │
│ OpenAI API (direct)                                        │
│   Orchestrator agent (GPT-5)                               │
│     └─ Same 5-specialist + chief pattern                   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                            ↓
               [ Central evaluation database ]
                            ↓
                    [ R analysis pipeline ]
```

### 6.2 Claude Code implementation
- Orchestrator runs as the main Claude Code session. For each manuscript:
  1. Loads the blinded JATS/markdown input from corpus directory
  2. Spawns 5 specialist sub-agents via the Task tool, in parallel, each with a locked system prompt
  3. Collects structured JSON outputs
  4. Passes all 5 outputs + the manuscript to a Chief Reviewer synthesis call
  5. Writes final combined JSON to `/results/<model>/<paper_id>.json`
- Implementation: a Python driver script invokes Claude Code in headless mode per paper; sub-agent prompts loaded from a frozen `/prompts/` directory.
- Both Opus 4.7 and Sonnet 4.6 runs use identical sub-agent prompts; only the orchestrator model differs.

### 6.3 Antigravity implementation (Gemini 3.1 Pro)
- Orchestrator + 5 specialists implemented as parallel agents in Antigravity's multi-agent workflow.
- Output schema identical to the Claude Code schema.
- Exported as JSON to `/results/gemini-3-1-pro/<paper_id>.json`.

### 6.4 GPT-5 implementation
- Direct OpenAI API calls with the same orchestrator + 5-specialist pattern implemented in a Python script (`openai_runner.py`).
- Output to `/results/gpt-5/<paper_id>.json`.

## 7. Rubric (common to agents and human reviewers)

Each dimension: 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent), plus free-text justification (2–5 sentences):

1. Methodological rigor
2. Statistical appropriateness
3. Level of evidence (Oxford CEBM 1–5)
4. Clinical / translational relevance
5. Novelty / originality
6. Reporting-standard adherence (CONSORT / STROBE / REMARK / PRISMA / ARRIVE / TRIPOD, as applicable)
7. Writing and presentation
8. Data & code availability / reproducibility

Chief Reviewer synthesis:
- Overall quality (1–10)
- **Highest tier at which this manuscript would likely be accepted** (1–4)
- Recommendation *at that tier* (accept / minor revisions / major revisions / reject)
- Key strengths and concerns

## 8. Preprocessing & Blinding Pipeline

The entire study rests on models never learning where a manuscript was published. Every manuscript must be transformed into a uniform, generic pre-submission format with all journal identifiers removed.

### 8.1 Source format
- **Primary**: JATS XML from PMC-OAS (clean, structured; enables deterministic extraction)
- **Fallback**: HTML/PDF from publisher APIs with structured-markup parsing + manual QA

### 8.2 Extraction & preservation
From each source, extract and preserve:
- Title
- Abstract (unstructured; journal-specific structure dropped)
- Introduction / Background
- Methods
- Results
- Discussion
- Conclusions
- Figures and tables (content; not journal-styled captions)
- Reference list (content; re-formatted to uniform Vancouver numbered style without PMIDs)

### 8.3 Strip list
The following **must** be removed or normalized:

| Category | Items |
|---|---|
| Journal identifiers | Journal name, ISSN, volume, issue, DOI, URL, running headers, "Published in…", copyright lines, publisher logo / watermark |
| Dates / history | Received / revised / accepted / published-online dates, article-history block |
| Journal-templated sections | Structured summary (Lancet), "Context" statement (JCO), editor's summary (Nature), "Research in context" box, "Capsule Summary", journal-specific graphical abstracts |
| Funding / acknowledgments | Remove — frequently identify institutions & funding mechanisms that correlate with journal |
| Conflict-of-interest disclosures | Remove — journal-form language leaks journal identity |
| Author affiliations | Remove all affiliations, author order, corresponding-author info, email, ORCID |
| Author names | Remove entirely (author-blind) |
| Data / code availability | Remove journal-templated sections; preserve only content-relevant data-availability text that does not name the journal |
| Supplementary material | Normalize labels ("Supplementary Material" → "Additional Materials"); remove journal-specific pointer URLs |
| In-text self-identification | Regex sweep for journal names (curated list of all oncology journal names), publisher names, "as reported in [Journal]" phrasing within the manuscript body |
| Reference artifacts | PMIDs, DOIs in references that could leak publication venue of citing paper (via PMC cross-links) — strip or neutralize |

### 8.4 Output format
All manuscripts rendered into a **standardized generic pre-submission template**:
- Markdown file (`.md`) following a fixed template
- Sections in fixed order: Title · Abstract · Introduction · Methods · Results · Discussion · Conclusions · References · Figures · Tables
- No headers/footers, no journal branding, no section numbering that reveals journal style
- Figure/table captions uniformly formatted
- References in Vancouver numbered style, no PMIDs

### 8.5 QA pipeline
For every manuscript, two automated checks must pass before inclusion in the corpus:

1. **Journal-name sweep**: regex search of the output against a curated list of ~400 oncology-related journal names (full + abbreviated forms, ISO standard + common variants). Any match → manuscript rejected or manually cleaned.
2. **Publisher / DOI sweep**: regex for DOI patterns, publisher names ("Elsevier", "Wiley", "BMJ", "Springer Nature", "AACR Publications", "ASCO", etc.) and publisher URL stems.

A 10% random sample undergoes manual QA by a research assistant before corpus freeze to validate the automated pipeline.

### 8.6 Contamination-probe integrity
The contamination probe (§11) is asked **before** the main review, so if the model does recognize the paper by content alone, this is logged and the paper's evaluation is flagged. Papers flagged as recognized by ≥ 1 model are analyzed separately (sensitivity analysis).

### 8.7 Pipeline deliverable
A Python package (`oncoblind`) implementing §8.1–§8.5, taking a PMID or PMCID as input and returning the normalized markdown + a QA report. Open-sourced at project-end.

## 9. Ground Truth

### 9.1 Primary ground truth: Actual journal tier
For each paper, the true tier (1–4, plus control tier 5) is known from the original publication. LLM-predicted "highest tier at which manuscript would likely be accepted" compared against actual tier via:
- Exact-match accuracy
- ±1-tier match accuracy
- Weighted κ (quadratic weights)

### 9.2 Gold-standard ground truth: Blinded expert re-review (n = 100 subset)
- **Two board-certified oncologists**, ≥ 5 years post-training, blinded to actual journal and all LLM outputs
- Same rubric as LLM agents (§7)
- Third senior reviewer adjudicates disagreements
- Human inter-rater agreement reported as ceiling benchmark
- Stratified sample: 20 per tier × 5 tiers

### 9.3 Exploratory ground truth
- Retraction status at 12 and 24 months (Retraction Watch)
- Citation trajectory at 24 months (Scopus / Dimensions)

## 10. Outcomes

### 10.1 Primary
1. **Weighted κ** between each model's "highest tier of acceptance" and actual journal tier, across the full corpus.
2. **Weighted κ** between each model's overall quality score and expert consensus, on the n = 100 subset.

### 10.2 Secondary
1. Inter-model agreement (ICC, 2-way mixed, absolute agreement) across Opus 4.7, Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.
2. Single-agent vs. multi-agent comparison within each model (paired).
3. Leniency: mean LLM-predicted tier vs. actual tier (paired t-test / Wilcoxon).
4. Temporal stability: 3 runs of same paper, same prompt → ICC.
5. Domain-specific agreement: methodological-agent flags vs. expert-flagged methodological issues.
6. Oxford LoE classification agreement (weighted κ) vs. expert LoE.
7. Hallucination rate: fabricated citations, statistics, or methodological claims, manually coded on a random sample of n = 50 outputs per model.

### 10.3 Exploratory
1. Retraction prediction (sensitivity/specificity at 12 and 24 months).
2. Citation-trajectory prediction.
3. Qualitative error taxonomy (thematic analysis).
4. Performance by model on contamination-flagged vs. non-flagged papers.

## 11. Contamination Controls

1. **Cutoff-based corpus restriction**: all papers published on or after Feb 1, 2026 (post Claude Opus 4.7 cutoff). Gemini 3.1 Pro (Jan 2025 cutoff) is more conservatively blinded — a full year of post-cutoff content.
2. **Pre-review recall probe**: before evaluation, the orchestrator asks each sub-agent "Have you previously encountered this manuscript, a preprint version of it, or closely-related work from the same authors? Answer yes/no with one-line justification. Do not use any such prior knowledge in the subsequent review." Logged in every output.
3. **Pre-cutoff validation subsample** (n ≈ 50, published 2024): evaluated in parallel to quantify contamination-related inflation of accuracy. Serves as an upper-bound contamination benchmark.

## 12. Statistical Analysis Plan

### 12.1 Primary
- Weighted Cohen's κ (quadratic) with 1,000-iteration bootstrap 95% CI
- Gwet's AC1 reported alongside to mitigate prevalence-imbalance paradox
- Accuracy (exact + ±1) with Wilson 95% CI

### 12.2 Comparative
- McNemar's test for paired binary accuracy (single vs. multi-agent)
- Ordinal mixed-effects logistic regression (paper = random effect; model, architecture = fixed effects) for Likert/tier outcomes
- Bland-Altman for 1–10 continuous quality-score comparisons

### 12.3 Subgroup
- By tier, study type, cancer domain
- By contamination-flag status (from §11)

### 12.4 Multiplicity
- Holm-Bonferroni within primary hypothesis family
- Exploratory outcomes reported with unadjusted CIs, explicitly labeled as hypothesis-generating

### 12.5 Software
- R 4.4+ (`irr`, `psych`, `ordinal`, `lme4`, `boot`, `BlandAltmanLeh`, `DescTools` for Gwet's AC1)
- Analysis code version-controlled; frozen at preregistration

## 16. Anticipated Limitations

- Journal tier as ground truth is noisy (reflects editorial decisions, not only quality)
- English-language only
- PMC-OAS restriction may skew corpus toward lower-tier and fully-open-access journals; partially mitigated by tier-stratified sampling and institutional-access fallback
- Training-data contamination cannot be fully excluded (models may have seen preprints or related work)
- Results specific to model versions frozen at study time
- Two expert re-reviewers is a small sample — we report agreement, not absolute accuracy
- Oncology-only — generalizability to other specialties unknown
- Prompt sensitivity: results conditional on frozen prompts
- Bias analyses (affiliation, author) deliberately out of scope; noted as future work

## 17. Novel Contributions

1. **First oncology-specific benchmark** for LLM-based manuscript evaluation.
2. **First evaluation of 2026 frontier models** (Claude Opus 4.7, Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5) in peer-review contexts.
3. **First factorial comparison of single-agent vs. multi-agent architectures** in scientific peer review.
4. **Fully-automated contamination-controlled corpus** via post-cutoff PMC-OAS restriction.
5. **Open-source reproducible blinding pipeline** (`oncoblind`) — reusable for future peer-review AI research.
6. **Oncology-calibrated rubric** integrating Oxford LoE, CONSORT/REMARK/STROBE/PRISMA/ARRIVE adherence, and domain-appropriate endpoint evaluation.
7. **Quality measure decoupled from reference journal** ("highest tier of acceptance") enabling pure content-based quality assessment.
