# Demo Blinded Manuscript Format

This synthetic file illustrates the approximate markdown structure expected by
the reviewer prompts. It is not derived from any real article.

## Title

Association of Example Biomarker X With Treatment Response in Advanced Example
Cancer

## Abstract

Background: Example biomarker X has been proposed as a candidate predictor of
response to therapy Y.

Methods: We conducted a retrospective cohort study of adults with advanced
example cancer treated at three centers between 2020 and 2024. Biomarker status
was measured before treatment. The primary endpoint was progression-free
survival. Cox regression was adjusted for age, performance status, disease
stage, and treatment line.

Results: Among 312 eligible patients, 118 had biomarker-positive tumors.
Biomarker-positive status was associated with longer progression-free survival
after multivariable adjustment. Sensitivity analyses showed similar direction
of effect, but confidence intervals widened in subgroup analyses.

Conclusions: Biomarker X may identify patients more likely to benefit from
therapy Y. Prospective validation is required.

## Introduction

Example cancer remains difficult to treat after progression on first-line
therapy. Existing clinicopathologic markers incompletely predict benefit from
therapy Y. Biomarker X is biologically plausible because it participates in a
pathway targeted by therapy Y.

## Methods

We included consecutive adults with histologically confirmed advanced example
cancer who initiated therapy Y during the study window. Patients lacking
baseline biomarker testing or follow-up data were excluded. The exposure was
biomarker-positive versus biomarker-negative status. The primary endpoint was
progression-free survival. Secondary endpoints included overall survival,
objective response, and adverse events.

## Results

The final cohort included 312 patients. Baseline characteristics were generally
balanced, although biomarker-positive patients were slightly younger. Median
follow-up was 18 months. Biomarker-positive status was associated with improved
progression-free survival in unadjusted and adjusted models. Missingness was
low for core covariates but higher for exploratory laboratory markers.

## Discussion

This synthetic example demonstrates the blinded manuscript format used by the
benchmark. In a real blinded input, author names, journal identifiers,
affiliations, dates, funding statements, acknowledgements, DOIs, URLs, and
publisher-specific article metadata would be removed before model evaluation.
